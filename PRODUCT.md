## Users

**Primary: hiring teams.** Engineering managers, technical recruiters, and founders/CTOs at AI and product companies, evaluating Abdullah for a senior engineering role. They arrive from a LinkedIn profile link, a GitHub profile, or a resume, usually mid-screening with several candidates open. Their job is to decide in well under a minute whether he is worth a conversation, and then to reach him without hunting for the address.

**Secondary: anyone who wants to connect.** Engineers who found him through open source (Maskode), hackathon showcases, or the AI/agents community. Same job, different motive: confirm the work is real, then get in touch.

The two readers differ in depth, not in intent. A non-technical recruiter checking role fit and an engineer checking whether the AI work is production or demo must both be served by one page, without a separate track for either.

## Product Principles

1. **Evidence over adjectives.** Every claim resolves to a shipped system, a live URL, a repo, or a stated figure from the resume. If it cannot be pointed at, it does not appear.
2. **One page, two readers.** A recruiter skimming for role fit and an engineer probing technical depth are served by the same scroll — depth is layered, not hidden behind a second destination.
3. **Production, not prototypes.** The through-line of every section is that models and agents reached real users. Where a project is a hackathon build, it says so plainly; the credibility comes from the engineering, not from inflating the context.
4. **Connecting is never more than one action away.** The contact path is explicit, direct, and repeated — links only, no forms, no funnels.
5. **Absence is designed around, never filled in.** Missing photo, screenshots, and social proof shape the design rather than prompting invention.
6. **Adding the next project stays cheap.** Content structure outlives this build; the site must not decay into stale hand-maintained markup.
